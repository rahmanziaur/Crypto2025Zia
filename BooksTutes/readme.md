# ICT 4115: Cryptography and Cyber Law

### B.Sc. (Engg.) in ICT | Fall Semester | MBSTU

Welcome to the course resource repository for **Cryptography and Cyber Law (ICT 4115)** offered to students of the Department of Information and Communication Technology (ICT), Mawlana Bhashani Science and Technology University (MBSTU), Tangail, Bangladesh.

This repository provides supplementary lecture materials, online courses, textbooks, lecture notes, and other learning resources to help students understand cryptographic principles, security mechanisms, and their applications in modern computing systems.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By following the course materials and recommended resources, students should be able to:

- Explain the fundamental principles and objectives of cryptography.
- Apply mathematical concepts used in cryptographic algorithms.
- Understand symmetric-key and public-key cryptography.
- Explain cryptographic hash functions, message authentication, and digital signatures.
- Understand key exchange, key management, and public-key infrastructure (PKI).
- Analyse cryptographic protocols and their security properties.
- Explore post-quantum cryptography and emerging cryptographic techniques.
- Understand the legal, ethical, and professional responsibilities associated with cybersecurity and cryptographic technologies.

## 📚 Recommended Learning Resources

The following resources supplement classroom lectures. Students should use them to review concepts, study examples, practise mathematical problems, and explore topics in greater depth.

### 1. Purdue University — Cryptography Lectures

**Resource:** [CS 555: Cryptography — Lectures, Handouts and Homeworks](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/jblocki/courses/555_Spring21/lectures.html)

This course provides lecture materials covering foundational and advanced cryptographic concepts.

Topics include:

- Classical ciphers and perfect secrecy
- Computational security and pseudorandomness
- Stream and block ciphers
- Chosen-plaintext and chosen-ciphertext security
- Message authentication codes and hash functions
- AES and other symmetric encryption techniques
- Number theory, RSA, and discrete logarithms
- Public-key encryption and digital signatures
- Zero-knowledge proofs and multiparty computation

**Recommended use:** Consult the relevant lecture notes when studying cryptographic definitions, algorithms, and security analysis. The course is an additional reference, not a replacement for the MBSTU lecture schedule.

### 2. Stanford University — Online Cryptography Course

**Resource:** [Online Cryptography Course — Dan Boneh](https://crypto.stanford.edu/~dabo/courses/OnlineCrypto/)

This free online course provides video lectures, presentation slides, and supplementary learning materials.

Topics include:

- Introduction to cryptography and stream ciphers
- Block ciphers and modes of operation
- Message integrity and cryptographic hash functions
- Authenticated encryption
- Key exchange and public-key encryption
- RSA and ElGamal cryptosystems
- Digital signatures and related constructions

**Recommended use:** Watch the relevant videos after attending classroom lectures. Use the slides to revise key concepts and the accompanying textbook for deeper explanations and exercises.

**Textbook:** [A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography — Dan Boneh and Victor Shoup](https://crypto.stanford.edu/~dabo/cryptobook/)

### 3. Cryptography 101 — Alfred Menezes

**Resource:** [Cryptography 101: Lecture Notes](https://cryptography101.ca/lecture-notes/)

This collection includes lecture notes and supplementary materials on several important cryptographic topics.

Recommended materials include:

- [Public-Key Cryptography](https://cryptography101.ca/lecture-notes/) — introductory concepts, encryption, and digital signatures.
- [RSA and Elliptic-Curve Cryptography](https://cryptography101.ca/lecture-notes/) — mathematical foundations and public-key techniques.
- [Post-Quantum Cryptography](https://cryptography101.ca/lecture-notes/) — cryptographic techniques designed to address threats from quantum computers.
- [Lattice-Based Cryptography](https://cryptography101.ca/lattice-based-cryptography/) — lattice problems, Learning With Errors (LWE), Short Integer Solutions (SIS), and quantum-resistant cryptographic schemes.

**Recommended use:** Use the introductory notes for public-key cryptography and the post-quantum materials for supplementary study of modern cryptographic developments.

### 4. MIT — Cryptography Course Resources

**Resource:** [MIT 6.875 — Cryptography](https://mit6875.github.io/)

Consult the course website for available books, lecture materials, and supplementary resources. Use these materials to reinforce your understanding of cryptographic theory and its applications.

**Recommended use:** Explore the available books and resources that match the topics discussed in class. Some materials may assume prior knowledge of discrete mathematics, probability, or theoretical computer science.

### 5. NPTEL — Cryptography and Network Security

**Resource:** [Cryptography and Network Security — IIT Kharagpur](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/106105162)

This course by Professor Sourav Mukhopadhyay of IIT Kharagpur provides a structured series of lectures on cryptography and network security.

Topics include:

- Classical cryptography and cryptanalysis
- DES, AES, and stream ciphers
- Number theory and modular arithmetic
- RSA, Diffie–Hellman, and ElGamal
- Elliptic-curve cryptography
- Message authentication and digital signatures
- Key management and cryptographic hash functions
- Cryptanalysis and implementation attacks
- Advanced encryption techniques and security applications

**Recommended use:** Follow the video lectures corresponding to the topics covered in class. Work through the mathematical examples and revise the concepts before examinations.

## 📖 How Students Should Use These Resources

1. **Before class:** Review the assigned textbook sections or introductory material.
2. **During class:** Take notes on definitions, algorithms, mathematical derivations, and worked examples.
3. **After class:** Revisit the corresponding online lecture or textbook chapter.
4. **Practise:** Solve mathematical problems and work through cryptographic examples independently.
5. **Revise:** Summarise important algorithms, their security assumptions, limitations, and applications.
6. **Explore:** Use advanced resources to investigate emerging topics beyond the introductory syllabus.

## 💻 Practical Learning and Academic Integrity

- Implement educational cryptographic examples in a controlled learning environment.
- Use small, illustrative inputs to understand encryption, decryption, hashing, and digital signatures.
- Never use classroom exercises to access systems, accounts, or data without authorisation.
- Do not treat an algorithm as secure merely because it successfully encrypts or decrypts a message.
- Follow the course instructions for assignments, laboratory activities, citations, and collaboration.

## ⚖️ Cyber Law

Cryptography and Cyber Law also concerns the legal and ethical dimensions of information security.

Students should follow the official course lectures and prescribed materials for topics such as:

- Privacy and data protection
- Cybercrime and unauthorised access
- Electronic records and digital signatures
- Digital evidence and its admissibility
- Intellectual property and responsible technology use
- Applicable cybersecurity and cybercrime legislation in Bangladesh

Use current official legal texts and amendments when studying legal provisions. Online cryptography courses are supplementary resources and do not replace the prescribed cyber law materials.
